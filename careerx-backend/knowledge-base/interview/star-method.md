# The STAR Method for Behavioral Interviews

STAR stands for Situation, Task, Action, Result. It's the most reliable structure for answering behavioral interview questions like "Tell me about a time you faced a conflict in a team project."

## Structure
- **Situation**: Set the context briefly — what was the project, team, or challenge?
- **Task**: What was your specific responsibility or goal?
- **Action**: What did YOU specifically do? Use "I", not just "we".
- **Result**: What was the measurable outcome? Quantify it if possible (e.g. "reduced load time by 40%").

## Example Question
"Tell me about a time you had to learn a new technology quickly."

A strong STAR answer names the specific technology, the deadline pressure (Situation/Task), the concrete steps taken to learn it — documentation, tutorials, asking mentors (Action), and the outcome — shipped the feature on time, or what was learned from the attempt (Result).

## Tips for Indian Engineering Students
- Draw from college projects, hackathons (including SIH), internships, or open-source contributions if you lack full-time work experience.
- Keep each STAR answer to 1.5-2 minutes — long enough to be specific, short enough to hold attention.
